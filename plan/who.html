Who

Who we test with

Three groups matter. We pick one to read first; the others still get the study and are read separately.

Recommended first

Developers and technical decision-makers, 22 to 40

People who evaluate infrastructure. Greg, 28 August: "those are the people who will have purchasing power by the time we need brand."

Second

Gen Z end users, 18 to 29

The people F1R3Drive and F1R3Sky are for. Greg's Gen Z feedback is where "FireLang beats RoLang" came from; this is where we check it with strangers.

Third

Enterprise buyers

Nicholas's contacts. Older, UK-weighted, the people a systems integrator would otherwise front for. Read on their own; never pooled with the first group.

How the forms sort people

Two questions at the start, before any name or page appears: what best describes your work, and your age band. Everyone completes the study; we read the groups separately. The website study adds a third screener, "had you heard of F1R3FLY before today," because people who already know the brand answer differently and would blur the result (researchers call this the halo effect). The name study asks the same thing at the end, after the name has been seen.
